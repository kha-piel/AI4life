# AI Literacy

Help the team make informed AI decisions without hype or unnecessary jargon.

## Core Model

For the proposed use case, distinguish:

- deterministic software from probabilistic model behavior;
- training knowledge from retrieved or tool-provided information;
- fluent output from verified truth;
- prototype performance from production reliability;
- model capability from the quality of data, prompt, tools, and workflow around it.

## Workflow

1. Explain what the system receives, produces, and cannot directly know.
2. Identify likely failure modes: hallucination, bias, ambiguity, stale knowledge, prompt injection, privacy leakage, and automation overreach.
3. Match oversight to impact. Low-risk suggestions may need sampling; consequential decisions need verification, auditability, and a human decision owner.
4. Show how to validate outputs with examples, counterexamples, sources, or deterministic checks.
5. Compare the AI approach with a rules-based, search, statistical, or manual alternative.

Adapt depth and terminology to the audience. Use a concrete example from their project whenever possible.

## Output

Provide a short capability-and-risk map, practical operating guidance, and one check that demonstrates understanding. Do not imply that prompting alone fixes missing data, weak evaluation, or unsafe product design.
