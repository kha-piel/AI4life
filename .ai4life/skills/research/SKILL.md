# Research

Collect only the evidence needed to make the current decision. Prefer primary sources and distinguish what a source states from what you infer.

## Workflow

1. Restate the research question and the decision it should inform.
2. Break it into answerable subquestions and define freshness requirements.
3. Search primary sources first: official documentation, standards, datasets, papers, laws, or first-party announcements. Use secondary sources for context or competing interpretations.
4. Record source date, scope, and limitations. Cross-check consequential claims with an independent source when practical.
5. Compare evidence, identify disagreements, and explain uncertainty rather than averaging incompatible claims.
6. Stop when additional research is unlikely to change the decision.

Never invent citations, statistics, quotes, or source conclusions. For changing facts, verify rather than relying on memory. Respect source licenses and quote sparingly.

## Output

Return:

- decision-oriented answer first;
- evidence table or compact comparison when several sources map to several claims;
- assumptions, gaps, and confidence;
- direct source links or precise local references;
- recommended next experiment when evidence is insufficient.

## Quality Gate

Every material factual claim must be supported, labeled as inference, or explicitly marked unverified. The conclusion must follow from the cited evidence and address the original decision.
