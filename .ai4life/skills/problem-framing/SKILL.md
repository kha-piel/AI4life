# Problem Framing

Turn an idea into a testable problem brief. Do not start choosing technology until the problem and desired outcome are coherent.

## Workflow

1. Identify the primary user, their context, and the job they are trying to complete.
2. Describe the current pain with observable evidence. Separate facts from assumptions.
3. State the desired outcome as a change in user or system behavior, not as a feature.
4. Capture constraints: time, data, privacy, devices, integrations, budget, team skills, and demo conditions.
5. Define one primary success metric and the minimum acceptable threshold. Add guardrail metrics where failure could cause harm.
6. List the riskiest assumptions and propose the cheapest validation for each.
7. Draw the MVP boundary: must have, explicitly out of scope, and later possibilities.

Ask only questions whose answers could materially change the solution. If answers are unavailable, proceed with clearly labeled assumptions.

## Output

Produce a concise problem brief containing:

- user and context;
- problem statement and supporting evidence;
- desired outcome;
- success and guardrail metrics;
- constraints and non-goals;
- assumptions ranked by risk;
- next validation step.

## Quality Gate

The brief passes only if a reviewer can explain who has the problem, why it matters, what success means, and what the team will not build. Flag a feature disguised as a problem statement.
